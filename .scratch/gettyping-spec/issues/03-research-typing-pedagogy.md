# Research typing-pedagogy best practices for sequencing a beginner curriculum

Type: research
Status: resolved
Research branch: research/typing-pedagogy (merged to master)

## Question

The Learn Track needs a gated, stage-by-stage curriculum appropriate for beginners as young as 5. What does established typing-pedagogy guidance say about how to sequence key introduction (e.g. home row first, then reaching rows, then numbers/punctuation), how many keys to introduce per Stage, what kind of unlock threshold (accuracy vs. speed vs. both) is appropriate at a beginner level, and any age-specific considerations for very young learners (attention span, motor development, letter recognition before touch-typing)? Findings feed directly into [04-curriculum-outline.md](./04-curriculum-outline.md).

## Answer

Full findings: [research/typing-pedagogy.md](../research/typing-pedagogy.md).

Every source examined (typing.com, TypingClub, Type to Learn, Mavis Beacon, Read Write & Type) agrees on **home row first → top row → bottom row → shift/capitalization → punctuation → numbers/symbols last**, justified by economy of finger-travel from the resting position rather than by letter frequency.

**Keys per stage**: convention is roughly 2-3 new keys per lesson generally, tightening to 1 new key per lesson for the youngest learners (Read, Write & Type's model, ages 6-9) — no source recommends more than ~5 new keys in one beginner lesson. Within a stage, content should sequence single keys → key pairs → words → sentences, with weak keys recycled back into later stages rather than a strictly linear progression.

**Unlock threshold**: gate on **accuracy, not speed** — recommend ~90% accuracy to unlock the next stage (below typing.com's 95% default, to avoid over-gating young children), with WPM present only as a soft/contextual number at the earliest stages, ideally ratcheting the accuracy bar up gradually rather than a single fixed number for the whole track.

**Age-specific (5-7)**: keep stages completable in ~10-15 minutes (within the documented ~12-18 minute attention span for ages 5-6); prioritize keyboard/letter-position familiarity over strict no-look touch-typing discipline for the earliest stages (formal technique solidifies more reliably around ages 7-9); favor narrative/character framing and immediate, tightly-coupled feedback (unlocking the next stage) over competitive/leaderboard mechanics for young learners, with optional audio/spoken cues.
