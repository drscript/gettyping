14 — this summary is the substance of the decision, not decoration. ADR 0003 built adaptive generation on the promise that targeting weak keys beats generic practice, but nothing in the spec ever showed the Player that working — the Profile shifted silently in a table they never see. This is the first and only surface where a Player watches the adaptive targeting do its job, and it fits the pedagogy research's principle of rewards "tightly coupled to typing progress itself": the reward here is literally the evidence of progress.
A "session" is a client-side grouping, not a modelled entity — snapshot the top weak keys when practice begins, diff against the current Profile at finish. No sessions table, no schema change.
Why keys drop off: 14 made the Profile recency-weighted (counters decay on write) because lifetime totals meant a Player who had genuinely fixed q kept being served q — the Profile grew less responsive exactly as they improved. The decay factor is a tuned constant in the map's fog.

17 — this screen is what six other tickets were promising. "Personal history" was the stated reason 09 made scores.exercise_id nullable and carried an index on (player_id, created_at), and the reason 10, 13 and 15 persisted Scores rather than rejecting them. Until this ticket the spec had an index built for a query nobody had described. No schema change — those two pieces simply stop being speculative.
Why it is not one list. A reverse-chronological feed would set a Practice Attempt targeting q and z beside one targeting e and t as though the numbers related, and bury the Speed Test trend under rows from the Track that generates the most of them. A single WPM-over-time chart across everything would be worse still — one line moving for reasons that have nothing to do with the Player improving.
Why the Speed Test can have a trend line at all. 14 made its text immutable so its Leaderboard would stay meaningful — every ranked Player must have typed the same thing. That decision, taken to protect the board, is also the only reason a personal trend here is honest.
The 118 wpm entry is listed but not plotted. It failed 10's plausibility check, so it carries 15's existing "not ranked" marker — same wording, same refusal to explain — and is excluded from the line. A list entry is a row; a trend is an assertion about direction, and one outlier from a laptop that slept mid-Attempt would make that assertion false. Marked where it is a row, absent where it would be a claim.

For grown-ups

There's no account

GetTyping never asks for an email address, a password, or a date of birth, and stores no personal information. A player is just a nickname.

Progress lives in this browser

Because there's no account, there's also no way to recover progress. If you clear this browser's cookies, or switch to a different browser or device, your child starts over as a brand-new player. Old scores stay on the leaderboards but can't be reclaimed.

Nicknames are public

Whatever nickname is chosen appears on public leaderboards alongside scores, which is why the app steers away from real names and offers a list to pick from instead.

More than one child?

Use "Not you?" on the home screen to switch between players on this device. Each keeps their own progress and their own practice profile.

Sound

The app makes a quiet sound when a key is typed wrongly, and a short one when a stage is cleared. Nothing the sound tells you is missing from the screen, so muting it costs nothing. The speaker icon is on every screen, including mid-exercise.

Stuck on a stage

Each stage needs 90% accuracy to clear, and the bar doesn't move. If a child is stuck and getting miserable, you can let them past it — the stage stays unfinished, but the next one opens.

11 — a persistent route, not an interstitial. Information delivered before it means anything is information not delivered, and a pre-flow wall of text is worthless when the child is the one who opened the tab. So this is permanent and findable at Stage 9, while the one actionable fact — the no-real-names nudge — is inlined at the Nickname step instead.
13 — the override is adult-granted and the child never sees a skip button. A child offered an escape from effort takes it every time, and arrives at Stage 21 having learned nothing. Equally rejected: lowering the bar after repeated failure, which does something worse than move a number — it makes "cleared Stage 6" mean different things for different Players, corrupting the only progress signal Learn has. The judgment "my child is stuck and miserable" needs context the app doesn't have and is genuinely an adult's to make.
16 — what the granted row means. A stage_unlocks row reads "Stage n counts as resolved", not "Stage n+1 is available" — availability of the next Stage is then derived exactly as it is from a gate-clearing Score. That re-read is what lets this work on Stage 21, where the old reading needed a row pointing at a Stage 22 that doesn't exist. An override on Stage 21 completes the Track, and the completion screen looks identical either way, because a quieter "you were let through" screen would leak the override straight back to the child at the app's proudest moment.
